Album

Standard E · Heavy Strings
Cornell · Jazzmaster
Marshall JMP + Mesa Dual Rec
Bi-amped · Neve preamps
Recording source: Producer Michael Beinhorn confirmed Cornell played rhythm guitar on a Fender Jazzmaster with heavy strings, bi-amped through a Marshall 50W JMP half-stack and a Mesa/Boogie Dual Rectifier — each cabinet mic'd with a Shure SM57 and a vintage RCA ribbon mic into Neve 1058 Germanium preamps. The tone is less gainy than it sounds. The bi-amp blend (Marshall mid-grit + Rectifier low-end weight) gives it density without excess saturation. Thayil has noted Superunknown tracks use less gain than most people assume.
Tone Character

The Sound

Dark, heavy clean with controlled push — not a saturated gain tone. Density comes from bi-amped weight, not distortion. Think sludge with articulation. The arpeggiated intro should feel like notes hanging in fog, not ringing clean. Chorus adds width but stays subtle — not Black Hole Sun.

What to Aim For

Mid-forward, compressed, slightly gritty. Low end present but not flabby. High end rolled — no sparkle. The 6/4 time means each note needs weight and space. Playing touch matters more than any setting: heavy pick attack, let strings thud against frets on the lower notes.

Guitar · Yamaha SA2200
SA2200
Bridge · Full Humbucker
Bridge pickup in full humbucker mode — tone pots pushed down, not split. Unlike the Portishead guide (middle position, both coils split), this tone needs the bridge humbucker's full output and density. The semi-hollow body gives natural bloom closer to Cornell's Jazzmaster character than a solid body would. Tone rolled back before the drive pedal to remove top-end edge. Unmarked dials — all clock positions.

Amp · Frost Clapp 15
Frost Clapp 15
Clean · Pushed Harder Than Portishead
No gain stage — volume and master control clean headroom only. Volume set higher than the Portishead guide to give the preamp more working material from the drive pedal. EQ defeat ON — the EB-10 in the loop handles all tonal shaping, so the amp EQ is bypassed. Bright switch OFF. Tremolo OFF for this song entirely.

Tumnus character: More transparent than the 385 — adds mid presence and harmonic richness without as much of its own pedal flavour. The EB-10 mid boost becomes more important with the Tumnus since the pedal alone won't provide Marshall mid grunt. More polished result; slightly less raw.
Boss BD-2 Blues Driver
Gain · Level · Tone · 3 Knobs Only
Three knobs only — Gain, Level, Tone. Asymmetric clipping, naturally open and touch-sensitive. Slightly bright by nature — Tone needs careful management with humbuckers or it gets harsh through the Clapp. At medium Gain it produces gritty, articulate crunch that responds strongly to pick attack: dig in hard on the verse riff and it opens up; back off and it cleans. Most dynamically responsive of the five. Level above unity pushes the Clapp preamp, adding compression the BD-2 alone doesn't provide.

BD-2 with EB-10: The EB-10 high cut (−3dB, knob at 3) is critical with the BD-2 — without it the Tone control gets brittle through the Clapp. More aggressive and open than the 385 but less amp-like warmth. Best for the verse riff; intro arpeggios require a lighter right hand to keep the gain from getting untidy.
Mozztronics GD-1 Bonebreaker
Gain · Tone · Volume · Body · 5-Way Mode
Five controls — Gain, Tone, Volume, Body (mid voicing), and a 5-way Mode switch. Body knob: fully CCW = mid-scooped Muff-style, fully CW = flat TS-style. Made in Melbourne. Mode 3 (Guv'nor) gives the most accurate Marshall JMP character for this tone — aggressive, mid-forward British crunch. Mode 5 (Mozztronics AC30 special) is the alternative for more harmonic complexity. Unmarked dials — all clock positions.

Mode guide: Mode 3 (Guv'nor) = Marshall-voiced British crunch, most accurate to JMP character. Mode 5 = more complex harmonic texture. Avoid Mode 1 (TS808) — its mid-hump adds nasal quality the EB-10 then has to fight. Body at 2–3 o'clock keeps mids present without getting boxy or honky.
MXR Super Badass Distortion
Output · Distortion · Bass · Mid · Treble
Five knobs — Output, Distortion, Bass, Mid, Treble. Active 3-band EQ with ±15dB range. Highest gain ceiling of the five but dial Distortion back significantly — this is not a metal tone. The onboard EQ is powerful enough that the EB-10 mid boost can be reduced: let the MXR Mid knob carry that work and avoid stacking two mid boosts. Most versatile raw gain shaping but least amp-like. Best for the verse riff; less nuanced on the intro arpeggios. Unmarked dials — all clock positions.

EB-10 Preamp / EQ
Always Engaged
Each band has a toggle switch (▲ boost / ▼ cut) that sets direction, and a knob (0, 3, 6, 9, 12) that sets the amount in dB. 0 = no effect regardless of switch position. More aggressive mid boost than the Portishead guide — Cornell's tone is mid-forward and thick, the opposite of Portishead's scooped character. Note: if using MXR Super Badass, reduce EB-10 mid band to knob 0 and let the MXR Mid knob carry that work.
