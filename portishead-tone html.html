Guitar · SA2200
Yamaha SA2200
Middle · Both Coils Split
3-way selector to middle position (both pickups), both tone pots pulled up to engage coil split. Neck humbucker + bridge single-coil — airier, more open, closer in character to Utley's Gretsch Filter'Trons than either humbucker alone.

Why middle + split: Utley's Filter'Trons are lower-output, airier, and more single-coil-like than full humbuckers. Splitting both SA2200 pickups and running the middle position gets you closest — the neck split carries warmth, the bridge split adds definition without mud. Roll both volumes back to ~8 o'clock so the 385 stays on the warm edge rather than pushing into drive. Open to fully on for Glory Box solo.

EQ Defeat ON: With the EB-10 in the loop doing all tonal shaping, bypassing the amp EQ gives you a cleaner, more open signal path. The amp just amplifies — the EB-10 sculpts. If you want to use the amp EQ instead, see fallback section below.

Tube Tremolo

Portishead tremolo character: Slow and deep — it should feel like the note is breathing, not stuttering. Think spy-film, not surf rock. The tube circuit gives it natural ramp rather than square-wave chop, which is exactly right for this sound.
EQ · Walrus EB-10 · In FX Loop
EB-10 Preamp / EQ
Always Engaged
Each band has a toggle switch (▲ boost / ▼ cut) that sets direction, and a knob (0, 3, 6, 9, 12) that sets the amount. 0 = no effect, 12 = full 12dB — in whichever direction the switch is set. The two work together: switch first, then dial in amount.
Low Band · ~100Hz
▲ BOOST
▼ CUT
3
Switch ▲ BOOST · Knob at 3 · = +3dB
Modest low-end warmth and body. Semi-hollow already has natural resonance — don't overdo it.
Mid Band · ~800Hz*
▲ BOOST
▼ CUT
6
Switch ▼ CUT · Knob at 6 · = −6dB
The most important setting. Portishead tone is famously mid-recessed — this hollow, scooped quality is what separates it from a generic clean tone.
High Band · ~3–5kHz
▲ BOOST
▼ CUT
3
Switch ▼ CUT · Knob at 3 · = −3dB
Tames pick attack and upper-mid edge from the split coils. Vintage air rather than sparkle — don't overcut or the tone loses definition.
Boost Switch
OFF
Mid Trim Pot (internal)
Set to ~800Hz
Mid Q Trim Pot (internal)
Medium Q — broad cut
Mid trim pots: Open the pedal and set the mid centre frequency trim pot to approx 800Hz (middle of its range tends to land around there — tune by ear, sweeping for the nasal honk frequency and cutting it). Set Q trim to medium — you want a broad, musical cut, not a surgical notch.

*Note: If you want a slightly more modern Portishead character (Third-era), shift mid cut toward 1kHz and tighten the Q slightly.
Delay · UAFX Starlight Echo Station
UAFX Starlight
Tape EP-III · Always On
Space Echo / Echoplex character. Utley ran his signal through a Roland Space Echo preamp throughout Dummy. Tape wear and modulation are part of the tone — not an afterthought. Six knobs: top row Delay / Feedback / Mix, bottom row Division / Color / Mod.

Tape wear is the tone: Color/Wear at 2–3 o'clock is what makes this sound right. The repeats should feel warm and slightly degraded — not hi-fi digital echoes. Mysterons: push Feedback to 2–3 o'clock and Time slightly higher for a more ambient, wash-heavy sound.
Reverb · UAFX Golden Reverberator
UAFX Golden
Plate 140 · Always On
Studio plate reverb — dense, smooth, controlled. Portishead live reverb is present but not cavernous. Plate 140 over Hall 224. Delay feeds into reverb — echoes bloom into tails. Six knobs: top row Decay / Predelay / Mix, bottom row Bass / Treble / Mod.

9 O'CLK
Restrained — supports the delay tails, doesn't dominate
Bass
12 O'CLK
Flat — preserve warmth in the reverb tail
Treble
10 O'CLK
Slightly rolled — darken the reverb tail to match the tone
Mod
7 O'CLK
Fully off — plate needs no shimmer or wobble
Ordering note: Starlight → Golden is correct. Tape echo feeds the plate reverb — the echoes decay into reverb tails. Reversing this order (reverb into delay) creates a smeared, less defined sound that doesn't sit right in a mix.
