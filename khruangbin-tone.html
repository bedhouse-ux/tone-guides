Yamaha SA2200
Neck Pickup · Volume 7–8 · Tone 6–7
Speer plays a Fender Stratocaster — single coil neck pickup, rolled-back tone, flatwound strings. The SA2200's neck humbucker is lower-output than most, with that semi-hollow body adding the warmth and slight air that a solidbody can't replicate. It's the closest instrument in the collection to that clean, slightly dark, compressed character. Roll the tone knob to 6–7 — this is not a bright tone. The semi-hollow resonance also approximates the way flatwound strings soften the attack on a Strat.
Key Setup Note Speer's wah is always on, parked — he finds a sweet spot and leaves it there, only sweeping when he wants an expressive effect. Without a wah pedal in this rig, the EB-10 mid band does the same job: a narrow boost around 800Hz–1kHz permanently engaged replicates the cocked upper-mid hump of a parked Cry Baby.

Why this amp works

Speer's core amp is a Fender Deluxe Reverb — Class A-adjacent, single-ended character, clean but musical. The Clapp 15 at 9W Class A is the closest amp in the collection: same clean headroom philosophy, same harmonic signature. The Clapp's lack of a gain stage is actually an asset here — Khruangbin's tone has zero dirt. Any breakup destroys it.

03 EB-10 — Wah Substitute + Shaping
The Core Idea

Speer's Cry Baby is always on and parked at around 60–70% toe — this creates a permanent upper-mid hump (~800Hz–1kHz) that is fundamental to his tone. The EB-10's Mid band, boosted moderately with a narrow Q, replicates this effect. It is not a filter sweep — it is a static tonal colour that should stay on for the entire set.

EB-10 Rule

Toggle sets direction (▲ boost / ▼ cut). Knob sets dB amount on a 0–12 scale. Knob at 0 = no effect regardless of toggle position — neither toggle should show selected when the knob is at 0.

How to Use It

Speer uses his Cry Baby for expressive dynamic sweeps during solos — the Snow White covers this role. Keep sensitivity very low so it doesn't trigger on rhythm playing. It should only respond to deliberate hard picks or solo phrases. The static mid-hump from the EB-10 and the dynamic envelope of the Snow White together approximate both roles of the Cry Baby. For most of the set, the Snow White does nothing — it's just there for solo phrases when you want the filter to open.

Delay Before Reverb

Run the Starlight before the Golden Reverberator in the effects loop. Delay into reverb gives the repeats their own spatial wash — correct for this sound. Reverb into delay smears the reverb tail into a murky mess and loses the dub-influenced precision of Speer's echo.

06 Playing Notes — By Track
Intro Riff
Single-note lines, neck pickup. Minimal attack — finger-pick or very soft plectrum. The EB-10 mid boost gives the notes that nasal, slightly hollow character without needing to do anything special with technique.
Chord voicings
Speer uses a lot of open-position and spread voicings — avoid dense barre chords. Let strings ring and decay naturally. The tape echo fills the space; don't over-play to compensate.
Dynamics
Everything is played quietly. The Clapp 15 at this volume setting will stay clean — any perceived warmth comes from the output transformer, not breakup. Resist the urge to dig in.
Delay interaction
Leave space for the Starlight repeats to breathe. Speer's phrasing is highly rhythmically spaced — he plays a phrase, lets the echo answer it, then plays again. Treat the delay as a second voice.
Funk-influenced picking
More percussive attack than Friday Morning. The Snow White autowah at very low sensitivity may just start to breathe here — don't increase sensitivity, let it respond naturally to the harder attack.
Muted string patterns
Light right-hand palm muting on chord stabs. The SA2200's semi-hollow resonance adds natural sustain — don't need to let everything ring. Short muted stabs create the percussive character alongside the bass.
Reverb level
Drop the Golden Reverberator mix slightly for this track — the percussive style needs less room. Increase the Starlight feedback slightly (to 4.5) for a more prominent echo on the off-beats.
Register
Mid-neck position, strings 2–4. The upper-mid emphasis from the EB-10 is most noticeable here — if it feels too honky, reduce the mid boost from 5 to 4.
Solo phrasing
Speer's solos are sparse, melodic, and highly vocal. Wide vibrato, slow bends. This is where the Snow White autowah comes into play — pick harder on sustained notes to trigger the envelope opening. Keep sensitivity low so it only activates on deliberate intent.
Delay time
Increase Starlight mix to 3.5–4.0 for solo sections — the echo becomes more prominent in the mix when playing melodic lines over a groove. Return to 3.0 for rhythm sections.
Pickup and volume
Keep SA2200 on neck pickup throughout. Guitar volume at 7 — rolling back slightly from 8 compresses the input to the Snow White and produces a more controlled envelope response.
Phrasing style
Think vocal melody, not guitar solo. Speer plays like he's singing. Long sustained notes, minimal fast runs. The EB-10 mid boost and tape echo together fill out the space a vocalist would occupy.
The Parked Wah Mindset
Speer's entire tonal identity is built around a wah that never moves. The EB-10 mid boost is a permanent colour, not an effect to turn on for solos. Leave it on all the time. This is the sound.
Flatwound equivalent
Speer uses flatwound strings — smooth, reduced string noise, darker high-end. With the SA2200 and tone rolled back to 6–7, the character is close. Don't use a pick with heavy attack; fingerpicking or a very light plectrum gets closer.
Volume control technique
Use the guitar's volume knob actively — roll back to 6 for clean rhythm comping, up to 8 for solo phrases. The Clapp 15 stays clean throughout; the SA2200 volume knob shapes dynamics, not the amp.
What not to do
No overdrive. No bright settings. No heavy picking. No fast runs. Khruangbin's guitar tone is defined entirely by restraint — in dynamics, in note choice, and in gain. Any dirt immediately kills the character.
