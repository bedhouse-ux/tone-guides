Source: Auerbach confirmed in 2019 Guitar World and MusicRadar interviews that Let's Rock was recorded primarily with a 1964 Guild Starfire III (mini-humbuckers) and a Gretsch Chet Atkins ("Rudy") through a Flot-A-Tone, a tweed Deluxe copy, and a silverface Deluxe Reverb. Fuzzes were a Marshall Supa Fuzz and an Ibanez Standard Fuzz — single-transistor character, not Big Muff. A Prescription Electronics Face Lift (octave channel), Uni-Vibe, and Catalinbread Belle Epoch delay rounded out the board. The tone is fundamentally cleaner, more dynamic, and spring-reverb-forward compared to the early records. The tremolo is a featured element on several tracks.
Tone Character

What Changed From Early Records

Let's Rock is not a Big Muff record. The fuzz is smaller, spikier, more single-transistor — treble booster territory rather than wall-of-sludge. The amps are cleaner and have natural spring reverb. The guitar changes frequently between songs. Much more dynamic range across the album.

The New Character

Think Gretsch-into-tweed-Deluxe-with-spring-reverb meets AC/DC chunk chords. Power and clarity simultaneously. Treble-boosted fuzz that bites rather than blooms. Some tracks are nearly clean. The tremolo wobble on tracks like Go is a defining feature, not background texture.

Guitar Choice
Primary
Postmodern Telecaster
The CuNiFe Wide-Range humbucker in the neck position is the closest pickup in your collection to the Guild Starfire III's mini-humbucker character — open, uncompressed, clear in the midrange, less dark than a PAF. The both-pickups position (Pos 2) adds the Nocaster single-coil bite to create an airy, articulate blend that approximates the Guild mini-humbucker sound more closely than either humbucker-heavy guitar. The Telecaster's natural attack and clarity also suit the treble-boosted, snappy fuzz character of Let's Rock better than the SA2200 or McCarty.
Alternative · Power Chord Tracks Only
Heritage H150 with SD '59s — closer to the Gretsch Chet Atkins ("Rudy") humbucker density for Shine a Little Light and Eagle Birds. Use bridge pickup, full open volume. The mahogany density suits those AC/DC-adjacent chunk chord riffs better than the Telecaster. Swap back to the Tele for everything else.

Guitar · Postmodern Telecaster
Postmodern Telecaster
Both Pickups · Primary Position
The both-pickups position (Pos 2) blends the CuNiFe WR humbucker warmth with the Nocaster single-coil snap — approximating the Guild Starfire III's mini-humbucker openness. This is the default position for most of the album. Switch to bridge only (Pos 1) for sharper, spikier fuzz tones. Tone slightly more open than the earlier guides — Let's Rock has more high-frequency clarity.

Why Both position: The Guild Starfire III's mini-humbuckers are lower output and more transparent than a full PAF. The both-pickups blend on the Postmodern Tele reduces output, opens up the high-end, and creates the airy, slightly hollow midrange character that's central to Let's Rock. Pure Nocaster bridge (Pos 1) is used for tracks where you want the Swollen Pickle to bite harder.
Always-On Drive · Wampler Tumnus Deluxe
Tumnus Deluxe
Treble Booster Role · Always On
Running as a treble booster — not an overdrive. Auerbach described "a single-transistor fuzz or maybe some sort of treble booster" as central to the Let's Rock snappy, explosive character. The Tumnus with low Gain, mid-to-high Level, and treble pushed does exactly this — adds presence, push, and harmonic shimmer into the Clapp's input without adding significant grit. Unmarked dials — all clock positions.

Treble booster vs overdrive: The Tumnus in this role should be nearly invisible as grit — what it adds is harmonic presence and a hotter signal into the Clapp's preamp. Fuzz · Way Huge Swollen Pickle
Swollen Pickle MkIIS
Single-Transistor Character · Not Always On
Engaged only for fuzz tracks — not always on unlike the early records. Let's Rock fuzz is smaller and spikier than Thickfreakness: Scoop near flat (CW), Filter brighter, Sustain lower. This approximates the single-transistor fuzz character (Marshall Supa Fuzz / Ibanez Standard Fuzz) Auerbach actually used — biting and aggressive rather than bloomy and sustained. Single-transistor vs Big Muff: The Swollen Pickle can approximate single-transistor fuzz character with Scoop near flat (2 o'clock), Filter at 12 o'clock, lower Sustain, higher Crunch, and Clip trimmer toward maximum. The result is spikier, more aggressive, and less bloomy than the Thickfreakness setting — it bites rather than sustains. The BD-2 is a good alternative for tracks needing even less fuzz commitment — just medium Gain into the Clapp.

Tremolo is now a featured element: Unlike the early records where the Clapp tremolo stays off, Let's Rock uses it prominently on several tracks. Auerbach used a silverface Deluxe Reverb specifically because it has tube tremolo (vibrato circuit) with a distinctive loping character. The Clapp's tube tremolo delivers the same effect — engage via footswitch and let it breathe.
EQ · Walrus EB-10 · In FX Loop
EB-10 EQ
Brighter Than Early Records · Mid Presence
Each band: toggle sets direction (▲ boost / ▼ cut), knob (0, 3, 6, 9, 12) sets dB. Let's Rock EQ is more open and present than the early records — the Swollen Pickle is no longer scooping aggressively, so the mid band is a more moderate boost. Low band left flat. High band cut is minimal — this record has clarity and presence that the early records deliberately avoided.

UAFX Golden Reverberator
Spring 65 Mode · New to This Album
Spring reverb is now present — Auerbach's silverface Deluxe Reverb has onboard spring reverb and it's audible on Let's Rock tracks. Unlike the early records (completely dry) and the Fell on Black Days guide (no reverb), the Golden is now in the chain. Use Spring 65 mode — not Plate. Short to medium decay, moderate mix. Adds the live, slightly splashy character of a small open-back combo's spring tank. UAFX Golden knobs — Decay, Predelay, Mix (top), Bass, Treble, Mod (bottom). All clock positions.

Spring 65 vs Plate 140: Spring 65 has the drip and splash of a physical spring tank — the slight wobble on the tail is correct for this. Plate 140 is denser and smoother, better for studio recording. Use Spring 65 here — it matches the Deluxe Reverb's onboard spring character Auerbach was actually using.

Playing Notes
Technique
More Dynamic · More Explosive · More Variable
Let's Rock is a more sophisticated album tonally than the early records. Dynamic range matters — the quiet passages need to be genuinely quiet so the loud ones land hard.
Pick Attack
Variable
Light touch when needed — the treble boost makes even light picking explosive
Power Chords
Dig In

Tremolo Feel
Let It Breathe
Don't fight the wobble — play to it, not through it
String Gauge
.010s
Lighter than early records — the Telecaster and treble boost reward lighter strings
Guitar Vol
Fully Open
Let the Tumnus boost do the work — don't choke the signal at the guitar
The Tumnus as treble booster: With the Tumnus always on at 8 o'clock Gain and 2 o'clock Level, even a light pick touch hits the Clapp's input hard. This is what Auerbach means when he says "touch the strings really light, but have the tone be explosive." The boost does the volume work — your dynamics control the character.
