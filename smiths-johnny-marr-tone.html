Sources: Marr's main live rig was Fender Twin Reverb + Roland JC-120 biamped, with Boss CE-2 Chorus as the primary effect. For the Smiths debut, a borrowed 1954 Fender Telecaster featured heavily. How Soon Is Now was recorded on a 1963 Epiphone Casino tuned to F# through a Twin Reverb (tremolo off for the initial tracking), then re-amped through four Fender Twin Reverbs all with tremolo engaged — tremolos set to 125bpm and 375bpm on separate stereo pairs. The slide was recorded on a 1955 Telecaster. Marr's jangle technique uses capos, open chord voicings with ringing strings, and layered guitar parts.
Tone Character

The Jangle

Clean, bright, shimmering. Marr never used distortion for The Smiths — the JC-120's chorus and the Rickenbacker's high-output single-coils created texture without dirt. The capo raises the register, open strings ring against fretted notes, and the result is a chiming, almost twelve-string character from a six-string guitar.

How Soon Is Now

Entirely different animal. The tremolo IS the song — not a texture added to a tone but the rhythmic and emotional foundation. Marr's tremolo pulse is urgent, hypnotic, slightly unsettling. The Clapp 15's tube tremolo at high depth, and understanding the two interlocking rates (125bpm and 375bpm on the original), is the whole challenge.

Postmodern Telecaster
Bridge Nocaster · Jangle Primary
Marr used Telecasters on much of The Smiths' studio work and Rickenbackers for the live jangle. The Postmodern Tele's '51 Nocaster bridge single-coil is bright, articulate, and cuts — the closest match to the Tele character Marr deployed on This Charming Man, There Is a Light, and What Difference Does It Make. The both-pickups position (Pos 2) adds a touch of warmth. Capo is essential — Marr regularly capoed 2–5 to raise the register and increase open-string ringing. Unmarked dials — clock positions.

Capo is the jangle secret: A capo on frets 2–5 raises the pitch and forces you to play open chord shapes in higher registers. Marr's chiming quality comes from open strings buzzing and ringing against fretted notes — something that only happens at higher positions. Without a capo the voicings are too low and the jangle disappears. Try any major chord shape with a capo at 5 and hear the difference.

EB-10 EQ
Treble Boost · Mid Presence · Boss GE-6 Role
Marr used a Boss GE-6 Graphic EQ on his pedalboard — primarily for boosting upper-mid presence and treble to enhance the jangle character. The EB-10 performs this role. The CE-2 character: The Boss CE-2 has a warm, slightly woozy chorus quality — not the pristine, transparent shimmer of modern chorus pedals. The Brigade's CE-1 or CE-2 mode captures this vintage character. If it sounds too clean or clinical, push the Depth knob to 1 o'clock. The chorus should add subtle movement and width to every strummed chord.

How Soon Is Now · Dedicated Setup
Recording facts: Marr tracked the rhythm on a 1963 Epiphone Casino tuned up a whole step to F# through a Fender Twin Reverb with tremolo OFF. Producer John Porter then re-amped the track through four Fender Twin Reverbs with tremolo ON — two per stereo side — tremolos running at 125bpm and 375bpm. This created two interlocking tremolo pulses creating the hypnotic, slightly unstable wavering. The slide was a borrowed 1955 Telecaster. "How Soon Is Now" is in F# — play standard E shapes with a capo on the 2nd fret.

SA2200 · How Soon Is Now

Frost Clapp 15 · Tremolo
Tube Tremolo · The Centrepiece
The Clapp 15's onboard tube tremolo is the reason this rig is the right tool for this song. Marr ran four Fender Twin Reverbs simultaneously at two different tremolo rates — 125bpm and 375bpm on separate pairs — creating an interacting, destabilised pulse. The Clapp 15 has a single tremolo with Speed and Depth controls. You cannot exactly replicate the dual-rate interlock with one amp, but a single well-set tremolo at the primary rate captures the spirit. Speed and Depth are the critical controls.
Primary Rate — Use This
125
BPM — the slower, driving pulse. The hypnotic heartbeat of the song. Set Clapp 15 Speed to this rate.
Secondary Rate (original recording)
375
BPM — 3× the primary. Second amp pair in the studio. Not replicable with single amp — the interaction created the unstable shimmer.

Setting tremolo to 125bpm: How Soon Is Now has a tempo of approximately 125bpm. Set the Clapp 15's Speed control so the tremolo pulse aligns with the quarter note beat — one complete pulse cycle every 480ms. Use a metronome or play along with the track to dial it in. The tremolo should feel like a heartbeat: steady, urgent, and slightly suffocating. Depth at 8–9 means the amplitude dip is dramatic — you should clearly hear the volume drop on each trough.

Starlight · Golden · How Soon Is Now
Ambient Wash · Spring Reverb · No Chorus
The original recording has significant reverb — the Twin Reverbs' built-in spring reverb combined with the re-amping process created a dense, ambient wash underneath the tremolo. No chorus (the tremolo replaces chorus as the modulation). Starlight provides light tape slapback for space. Golden in Spring 65 mode for the Fender spring character with moderate decay.

No chorus on this song: Switching the Brigade chorus off is non-negotiable for How Soon Is Now. The tremolo and chorus fighting each other destroys the hypnotic quality of the pulse. Marr Technique
Jangle · Open Strings · Capo · Layering
Marr's tone is 50% technique, 50% gear. The capo, open voicings, and picking style are inseparable from the sound.

Chord Voicings
6ths + 7ths
Major 6th (1-3-5-6) and maj7 voicings give the jazzy, shimmering quality
HSIN Rhythm
Steady Strum
For How Soon Is Now: play a steady rhythm and let the tremolo create the pulse — don't fight it
The capo insight: Marr has said that using a capo is like playing a different guitar. At capo 5, a standard G chord shape produces notes in the register of a 12-string — open strings that were previously below the tonic now ring higher and more brilliantly. This is the core of the jangle character. Every song should be explored with a capo at different positions until the right brightness and register is found.
