THE
BLACK KEYS

Thickfreakness · Rubber Factory — 2003–2004
Postmodern Telecaster Frost Clapp 15 Swollen Pickle BD-2 + GD-1 · 385 MKII
Source: Auerbach used an EHX Big Muff Pi on both Thickfreakness and Rubber Factory — confirmed as the pedal of that era before the Shin-ei Companion took over for Brothers and later. His amps were lo-fi workhorses: Magnatone, Fender Musicmaster bass amp, Victoria Dual Deluxe. The defining character of both records is a cranked, slightly saggy amp with a fuzz smeared across everything — raw, mid-scooped, bloomy, and unpolished. Recorded in a rehearsal space (Thickfreakness) and an abandoned rubber factory (Rubber Factory). The lo-fi aesthetic IS the tone.
Tone Character

What It Is

Big Muff through a small, slightly saggy amp at medium-high volume. Mid-scooped fuzz bloom. Notes hang and sustain into each other. The guitar fills all the sonic space the bass would normally occupy in a band — you're a two-piece. The tone has to be enormous.

What to Avoid

Too much high-end crunch, tight modern distortion, clean headroom. This tone should feel like it's slightly about to fall apart. The Clapp 15 pushed with a Swollen Pickle should get naturally compressed and slightly saggy — if it sounds polished, it's wrong.

Guitar · Postmodern Telecaster
Postmodern Telecaster
Bridge Nocaster · Full Tele Attack
Bridge pickup — the '51 Nocaster single-coil. Tight low end, clear mids, enough bite and twang to cut through a scooped fuzz. The CuNiFe humbucker in the neck position is too warm and thick for Thickfreakness — the single-coil bridge pickup feeds the fuzz harder and responds with the raw, aggressive snap this tone needs. Unmarked dials — clock positions.

Nocaster into fuzz: The '51 Nocaster bridge pickup has tight low end and clear mid-range that survives the Big Muff's mid scoop better than a humbucker would. Keep the tone rolled slightly — at fully open the Nocaster can get brittle feeding a Muff-style fuzz. The guitar volume at full lets the fuzz sustain bloom properly.
Drive · Boss BD-2
Boss BD-2
Pre-Fuzz · Always On · Low Gain
Always-on mild push before the Swollen Pickle. Low Gain — this isn't a dirt pedal here, it's a signal conditioner. Tightens the attack of the fuzz, sharpens the front edge of notes, adds a tiny bit of compression. Level slightly above unity so the Pickle sees a hotter signal. The tone of Thickfreakness is the Swollen Pickle — the BD-2 just makes it behave better.

BD-2 before a fuzz: Stacking a mild drive before a Muff-style fuzz is a classic trick — it cleans up the fuzz's flubby low end and makes individual notes articulate more inside the sustain. If anything, use the BD-2 alone (Pickle off) for lighter passages where you want grit without full fuzz commitment.
Fuzz · Way Huge Swollen Pickle
Swollen Pickle MkIIS
Loudness · Sustain · Filter · Scoop · Crunch
Five external knobs — Loudness, Sustain, Filter, Scoop, Crunch. Two internal trim pots — Voice (sets Scoop intensity) and Clip (clipping diode character). For Thickfreakness: Scoop toward CCW for classic mid-scoop (Big Muff territory). High Sustain. Filter in the woolly-dark zone. This is the biggest, most scooped fuzz setting of the two eras. All clock positions.

Scoop = CCW for Thickfreakness: The Scoop knob toward 9 o'clock with Voice at 3 o'clock gives you the deep, classic mid scoop of a Green Russian Big Muff — this is the sound. Crunch at 10 o'clock keeps the fuzz from gating and gives it that slightly saggy, velcro bloom. If the tone gets too woolly and undefined, nudge Filter CW slightly toward 11 o'clock.
Amp · Frost Clapp 15
Frost Clapp 15
Pushed Hard · No EQ · No Tremolo
No gain stage — volume and master control headroom only. Volume pushed high to generate natural preamp compression and slight sag when the Pickle hits. EQ defeat ON — EB-10 does the shaping. The amp should be borderline breaking up on its own when the fuzz is off, so that engaging the Pickle tips it into full saturation.

Class Mode
Class A · 9W
Push the Clapp harder here: Auerbach's amps were small and cranked — a Fender Musicmaster bass amp or Victoria Dual Deluxe at high volume. The Clapp 15's Class A 9W output is closer to those amps than any large clean platform. At pre-volume 9–10 it should feel responsive and slightly strained — that's correct.
EQ · EB-10 · In FX Loop
EB-10 EQ
Post-Fuzz Shaping · Always On
Each band: toggle sets direction (▲ boost / ▼ cut), knob (0, 3, 6, 9, 12) sets dB amount. The Swollen Pickle in mid-scoop mode removes mids and adds heavy low end. The EB-10 is used here to restore some low-mid body and tighten the low end — the Pickle alone can get flabby. High band cut to reduce Nocaster bridge bite that survives the fuzz.

Low cut with the Pickle: Counterintuitive — but cutting the low band slightly (−3dB at 100Hz) when using a scooped Muff-style fuzz actually makes the bass feel bigger and tighter, not smaller. You're removing the uncontrolled sub-bass bloom that makes a Muff feel flabby. The mid boost compensates for what the Scoop knob removes.

Alternative — 385 MKII: If you prefer a warmer, more amp-like push before the Pickle, use the 385 MKII Side A at 10 o'clock gain with 385+ ON. Playing Notes
Technique
Blues · Two-Piece · Fills All Space
As a two-piece, the guitar has to fill all the sonic space a bass, rhythm guitar, and lead would normally share. That's why the fuzz is so big — you need to occupy the room. Everything you play matters more because there's nowhere for weak notes to hide.
Pick Attack
Aggressive
Dig in hard — the fuzz rewards heavy pick attack
Riff Style
Sparse
Leave space — the fuzz sustain does the work between notes
Guitar Vol
Wide Open
Full volume into the fuzz — let the Pickle react to the full signal
Blues Feel
Mississippi
Raw Delta and hill country blues — not polished Chicago style
String Gauge
.010–.011s
Heavier strings respond better to the aggressive attack and give the fuzz more to work with
The lo-fi aesthetic: Don't try to make this sound polished. Auerbach recorded Thickfreakness in one day on an 8-track reel-to-reel. If your tone sounds like it was produced in a professional studio — you've gone too far. The slightly broken, barely-held-together quality of the Swollen Pickle under a cranked Class A amp is exactly right.
